# Sunseeker and Princess yacht range research

Checked: 14 September 2026  
Scope: current/new-market models plus previous-generation models produced or sold at some point from 2006 through 2026. The table is model- and major-variant level, not every hull number, engine package, refit, or regional trim.

## Company context

Sunseeker began in Poole in 1969 as Poole Power Boats and adopted the Sunseeker International name in 1985. Its current public range spans fast sports yachts, flybridge yachts, Ocean long-range yachts, and superyachts. Sunseeker’s authorized brokerage network describes the company as employing roughly 2,000 people, producing around 150 boats per year, and exporting about 98% of production; those figures are brokerage claims rather than audited manufacturer figures. In 2026, Sunseeker announced Cross Ocean Partners and Cheyne Capital as its long-term owners and shareholders.

Princess was established in Plymouth in 1965 as Marine Projects. Princess says more than 80% of each yacht is crafted in Plymouth; its 2025 corporate material states that it had more than 2,300 employees and 42 authorized distributors worldwide. Princess is part of the KPS Capital Partners portfolio.

## What the cross-source comparison shows

- The official model pages are the best source for current technical specifications, but the two brands do not keep every regional or dealer model synchronized with the global range landing page. Dealer pages may show models intended for the next delivery year, local-market variants, or separately named open/deck-saloon versions.
- Sunseeker’s biggest naming issue is the transition between “Sport Yacht” and the global page’s “SportFly” names, plus the Manhattan 94 being a newer name for the 94 Yacht. The 86 Ocean Enclosed page is currently internally inconsistent: its title says 86 while its published dimensions match the 82 Ocean Enclosed. The 82 Ocean plain model is dealer-listed but did not have a reliable detailed current specification in the sources checked.
- Princess has similar channel differences: the manufacturer’s global site, regional Princess sites, the authorized dealer, and independent YachtBuyer history do not expose exactly the same set at the same time. F54 is visible on Princess regional/dealer pages while F55 remains on the global page; V50 Open is separately marketed by the dealer; and S72/Y85 have regional or historical Princess pages even when they are absent from the current global grid. Those rows are labeled by source and era rather than being silently collapsed into one “current” list.
- Independent listings are useful for real-world checks but should not override the manufacturer’s technical sheet. For example, a YachtWorld 90 Ocean listing broadly matches Sunseeker’s 90 Ocean layout and engine family but has slightly different tank and dimensional values; broker listings themselves warn that specifications may not be guaranteed.
- Princess’s public model pages generally publish top-speed bands but not formal range figures. “Range not published” therefore means “not stated on the public source checked,” not that the yacht has no meaningful cruising range.
- The historical section intentionally uses a lighter spec density than the current section. Old boats were offered with multiple engines, layouts, tank configurations, and refits, so a single generic historical number can be misleading. For a purchase decision, the hull-specific builder plate, original brochure, survey, and engine hours should take precedence over a model-level archive.
